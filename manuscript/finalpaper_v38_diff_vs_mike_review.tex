\section{Introduction and Background}
Recent structure--property studies have linked spinodal decomposition to stiffening of \textit{bcc} $\delta$--ferrite in cast austenitic stainless steels (CASS), and have shown that this stiffening can be detected through picosecond ultrasonic surface acoustic wave (SAW) peak splitting \cite{al2023detecting, al2020detecting,al2020non}.
This paper establishes a quantitative theoretical foundation for predicting that SAW signature from first principles by combining elastodynamics with density functional theory (DFT) to connect composition, elastic response, and Rayleigh-wave observables.

For high--melting-point metals (including \textit{bcc} $\delta$--ferrite), elasticity is often closely tied to plastic response: dislocation energetics are largely stored in the Hookean lattice outside the dislocation core, and thermally activated processes during deformation can modify elastic properties \cite{pugh1954xcii}.
More broadly, cohesive properties quantified by lattice elasticity correlate with melting point through their shared dependence on interatomic bonding and electronic structure \cite{chang1966correlation,fine1984elastic, korzhavyi2009electronic, razumovskiy2011first, hosemann2018materials}.
In ferromagnetic Fe--Cr alloys, these quantities vary strongly with concentration \cite{korzhavyi2009electronic}, and the experimentally observed SAW peak splitting upon spinodal decomposition is consistent with an anomalous concentration dependence of elastic constants associated with electronic-structure effects (including Fermi-surface topology changes \cite{razumovskiy2011first}).

During spinodal decomposition of \textit{bcc} $\delta$--ferrite, chromium partitioning produces Fe-rich $\alpha$ and Cr-rich $\alpha'$ regions whose differing compositions imply differing stiffness. As a result, elastic stiffening can serve as a mechanistically grounded proxy for embrittlement in stainless steels undergoing thermal and/or irradiation-assisted aging \cite{du2017facile, sdajani2019cassspinodal,al2020non, hofmann2019transient, al2020detecting,byun2016thermal,lach2020precipitation}.
Because the Rayleigh SAW frequency depends directly on elastic moduli, picosecond ultrasonic pump--probe spectroscopy provides a direct, non-destructive route to infer stiffness changes and therefore track the progression toward embrittlement \cite{hofmann2019transient}.

Motivated by these links, we develop a thermodynamics-to-observable workflow for \textit{bcc} Fe$_{1-x}$Cr$_x$ as a model for $\delta$--ferrite: (i) we identify spinodally unstable composition windows using the curvature of the demixing free energy, (ii) we quantify the composition dependence of cubic elastic constants from DFT, and (iii) we map these elastic inputs into Rayleigh SAW frequency ranges using elastodynamic constraints relevant to picosecond ultrasonics. Finally, we separate a solid-solution trend $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}(x_{\mathrm{Cr}})$ from an evolution trend $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}(\phi)$ parameterized by the spinodal conversion fraction $\phi$ from \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{molecular dynamics (MD)}\DIFdelend \DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{our earlier atomistic simulations of decomposed Fe--Cr \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{al2023detecting}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
}\DIFaddend , enabling direct interpretation of SAW peak shifts and splitting in terms of composition and decomposition state.

Only the stiffest tensors enter it: Cr-rich literature alloys (9\% and 29\% of grains above 0.609 GHz at $x_{\mathrm{Cr}}=0.9$ and 1.0) and the present DFT tensors (30\% and 46\% at $x_{\mathrm{Cr}}=0.95$ and 1.0; their systematic overstiffening also places 6--8\% of grains of Fe and Fe$_{50}$Cr$_{50}$ in the range). Frequencies in this range therefore indicate stiffness beyond that of a homogeneous solid solution of $\delta$-ferrite or Fe--36Cr composition, consistent with the stiffer Cr-rich $\alpha'$ regions formed by spinodal decomposition (Fig.~\ref{fig:fsaw_combined}a). This agrees with the mechanism observed experimentally in Ref.~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{al2023detecting}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
, in which aging stiffens the $\delta$-ferrite until its surface-wave peak separates from that of the austenite (peak splitting in 60--100\% of aged spots against 0--25\% unaged; Table~3 of Ref.~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{al2023detecting}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
). Our calculations identify the stiffening that places peaks in the unallowed range: decomposition stiffens mainly the tetragonal shear modulus $C'$ (by 17--29\% for Fe--36Cr, through the Cr-rich $\alpha'$ phase) rather than $C_{44}$, so it cannot lift the top of the (P)SAW branch above about 585~MHz for any microstructure, but it lowers the elastic anisotropy and makes weak high-velocity pseudo-SAWs above 665~MHz, beyond any surface mode of pristine CASS, three to five times more frequent (QE: 0.4 to 1.9\% of grains; EMTO-CPA: 2.6 to 6.2\%), as in the measured tail (0.2\% of peaks above 665~MHz unaged against 2--15\% after aging at 290--400~$^\circ$C; Fig.~9 of Ref.~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{al2023detecting}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
; SM Section S9). At the electronic level, the three effects act on different elastic channels: the stiffer $C'$ of $\alpha'$ follows the filling of the }\textit{\DIFadd{bcc}} \DIFadd{$d$ band, which stabilizes the }\textit{\DIFadd{bcc}} \DIFadd{lattice towards Cr \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{soderlind1993theory} }\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
(QE: $C'$ rises from 56~GPa at 5\%~Cr to 145~GPa at 95\%~Cr); the electronic topological transition at $x_c\approx0.08$ (SM Section S6) sets the anomalous moduli of the Fe-rich $\alpha$ phase, which decomposition carries across it; and the spin- and charge-density waves of the Cr-rich $\alpha'$ couple to the volume rather than to shear (antiferromagnetic order lowers $K$ of Cr by 29\% but $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}$ by only 1\%; SM Section S9), so they lower $K/G$, Pugh's ductility measure \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{pugh1954xcii}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
, without affecting the acoustic signature. The same shear stiffening links the acoustic signal to embrittlement: decomposition raises $G$ (+11\% for MD Fe--36Cr) while leaving $K$ nearly unchanged ($-1.2\%$), so Pugh's ratio $K/G$ falls by 11\% \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{pugh1954xcii} }\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
in step with the SAW shift, $\Delta(K/G)/(K/G)\approx-2\,\Delta f/f$; meanwhile the shear-modulus misfit between $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ (+16 to +23~GPa from QE and EMTO-CPA) hardens the ferrite against dislocation motion \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{kato1981hardening}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
, while the cleavage resistance, which scales with $K$, stays fixed. Stiffening therefore shifts the balance from plastic flow towards cleavage, as in the Charpy energies of Ref.~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{al2023detecting}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
, which fall from 88.5~J to 14--24~J for CF8 aged 10}{\DIFadd{,}}\DIFadd{000~h at 330--400~$^\circ$C as the SAW splitting grows from 12 to 128--229~MHz (SM Section S9). These elastic changes act in the regime where Pugh's relations hold \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{pugh1954xcii}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
: for metals melting above about 900~$^\circ$C, thermally activated processes play a negligible part in room-temperature plastic deformation, so the stress needed to move a dislocation, and with it the hardness, scales with $Gb$ ($G$, shear modulus; $b$, Burgers vector), while fracture is set by the maximum of the stress--separation curve beyond its Hookean rise and scales with $Ka$ ($K$, bulk modulus; $a$, lattice parameter). Fe--Cr melts above 1500~$^\circ$C, so the shear stiffening on decomposition raises the resistance to dislocation glide, and hence to plastic flow, at an almost unchanged cleavage stress; the Cr-rich $\alpha'$, whose Debye temperature (the elastic scale of the melting point in the Lindemann relation $T_m\propto MV^{2/3}\Theta_D^2$, SM Fig.~S5 \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{fine1984elastic}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
) exceeds that of the random alloy by 10--18\% (QE, EMTO-CPA), lies deeper still in this athermal regime, and stiffening and embrittlement therefore advance together. }\DIFaddend Within the spinodally unstable composition window, elastic stiffening therefore translates \DIFdelbegin \DIFdel{directly }\DIFdelend into measurable acoustic signatures, enabling stiffness changes to be inferred non-destructively.

\section{Discussion}

Taken together, the results establish a closed thermodynamics-to-observable pathway for interpreting picosecond ultrasonic measurements in $\delta$--ferrite–containing stainless steels. The free-energy curvature criterion identifies the composition window in which the homogeneous solid solution is intrinsically unstable, defining the regime where elastic and acoustic properties must reflect ongoing or incipient spinodal decomposition rather than equilibrium alloy behavior.

Within this thermodynamically unstable regime, first-principles elasticity reveals systematic stiffening trends that are propagated through elastodynamic constraints into Rayleigh SAW frequency shifts. The direct mapping between elastic constants and SAW observables provides a quantitative basis for interpreting peak shifts and splitting as signatures of stiffness evolution, rather than purely phenomenological indicators.

Crucially, separating the solid-solution trend $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}(x_{\mathrm{Cr}})$ from the spinodal conversion trend $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}(\phi)$ resolves a key ambiguity in experimental interpretation. While composition alone can shift SAW frequencies, progressive spinodal decomposition introduces additional stiffening that cannot be captured by composition-dependent elasticity alone. The combined framework therefore enables SAW peak splitting to be interpreted as a mechanistically grounded indicator of microstructural evolution toward embrittlement, rather than as a purely compositional effect.

\DIFaddbegin \DIFadd{Consistent with experiment, the models reproduce the sign and composition dependence of the stiffening, although not its full size: aging raises the mean second TGS peak of CASS by 90--220~MHz (Table~3 of Ref.~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{al2023detecting}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
), whereas full decomposition raises the frequencies of binary Fe--Cr by 5--8\%. The models omit the Ni, Mn, Si and (in CF8M) Mo of CASS ferrite and its G-phase and carbide precipitates \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{byun2016thermal,lach2020precipitation}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
. Each 100--140~$\mu$m TGS spot also samples several ferrite and austenite grains, so the mean second peak mixes stiffening with which grain, phase or mode is detected, including the weak high-velocity pseudo-SAWs that decomposition makes more frequent, whereas we count modes rather than detected amplitudes. Finally, the DFT tensors describe 0~K, magnetically ordered rather than paramagnetic alloys and overstiffen the bulk modulus at the PBE volume, the empirical potential makes both phases strongly anisotropic, full decomposition is an idealized limit, and no linear-elastic average of a coherent microstructure can exceed the Voigt bound.
}

\DIFaddend This separation clarifies why SAW peak splitting emerges as a robust, non-destructive diagnostic of spinodal decomposition in CASS and related alloys, and suggests a general strategy for extending ultrasonic embrittlement detection to other phase-separating structural materials.


\section{Conclusions}
We presented a compact thermodynamics-to-elastodynamics workflow for bcc Fe--Cr $\delta$--ferrite that links demixing free-energy curvature, first-principles elastic constants, and Rayleigh SAW observables. Free-energy curvature provides a direct stability criterion that identifies spinodally unstable composition windows. DFT-derived cubic elasticity quantifies composition-dependent stiffening trends and supplies inputs for elastodynamic prediction. Combining these elements yields experimentally actionable relationships for interpreting picosecond ultrasonics across both composition and decomposition-state variables.

\subsection*{\DIFadd{S3. First-principles calculations: methods and overview}}
\paragraph{\DIFadd{Methods.}} \DIFadd{An independent set of first-principles calculations was performed with Quantum ESPRESSO 7.5 \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{giannozzi2009quantum,giannozzi2017advanced} }\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
on the MIT Engaging cluster, using PBE \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{pbe1996} }\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
and PAW potentials from pslibrary (Fe 3s3p3d4s, Cr 3s3p3d4s), cutoffs of 60/480 Ry (wavefunctions/density), and Marzari--Vanderbilt smearing of 0.02 Ry. The alloys are 40-atom SQS ($2\times2\times5$ conventional }\textit{\DIFadd{bcc}} \DIFadd{cells; pair correlations optimised over three neighbour shells) at $x_{\mathrm{Cr}}$ from 0.05 to 0.95 (Table~\ref{tab:S_structural_convergence}), sampled with a $4\times4\times2$ $k$-mesh. Starting moments are ferromagnetic, except Cr antiparallel to Fe at 80\% Cr and for 65\% Cr at the experimental lattice parameter, which converge more reliably from that start, and each cell is fully relaxed (box lengths and ions). Elastic constants follow from the stress response to strains $\varepsilon_{xx}=\pm0.5\%$ and $\gamma_{yz}=\pm0.5\%$ with ions relaxed at each strain (central differences). For Fe--80Cr the SCF of the $-\varepsilon_{xx}$ step did not converge below $2\times10^{-6}$~Ry, and a rerun at that threshold shifts all diagonal stresses by 3~kbar (measured on the $\gamma_{yz}$ steps, which converged at both thresholds and give the same $C_{44}$ to 0.2~GPa). There $C_{11}$ and $C_{12}$ come from the forward difference between $+\varepsilon_{xx}$ and the relaxed cell. The response is linear: the relaxed shear stress lies at the midpoint of the $\pm\gamma_{yz}$ stresses to 0.005~kbar. Pure Fe (FM) and Cr (NM and commensurate AFM) use 2-atom cells with $16^3$ meshes. Convergence for }\textit{\DIFadd{bcc}} \DIFadd{Fe: $C_{ij}$ change by $\leq2$ GPa between 60 and 80 Ry and are converged by a $12^3$ mesh. Electronic structure: atom- and spin-projected DOS from a non-self-consistent $6\times6\times3$ run with the optimised tetrahedron method; Fermi-surface sections on the $k_z=0$ plane; band structures along $\Gamma$--H--N--$\Gamma$--P--H. Phonons: density-functional perturbation theory on a $6^3$ $q$-grid, Fourier-interpolated along the same path.
}

\paragraph{\DIFadd{Overview.}} \DIFadd{The additional first-principles material is organised by topic: elastic constants at the PBE-relaxed and the room-temperature experimental lattice parameter (Section S4); electronic structure, including the unfolded Fermi surfaces and band structures of the Fe-rich alloys (S5); the electronic topological transition that links them to the Fe-rich elastic anomaly (S6); lattice dynamics (S7); a validation of every computed quantity against published data and experiment, including the thermal-transport estimates of Section S1 (S8); and the proposed mechanism figures (S9). Each section gives its text first and its figures after. The convergence of the elastic constants with Brillouin-zone sampling and cutoff is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:S_convergence}, and the structural convergence of every relaxation (residual forces and pressures) in Table~\ref{tab:S_structural_convergence} and Fig.~\ref{fig:S_structural_convergence}.
}

\subsection*{\DIFadd{S4. Elastic constants}}
\DIFadd{Figure~\ref{fig:S_fe_rich} compares the Fe-rich elastic constants with EMTO-CPA and MD. \DIFadd{At the experimental lattice parameter $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}$ rises from 446~MHz for Fe (EMTO-CPA: 449~MHz) to 485 at 20\%, flattens at 497, 500 and 499~MHz between 30 and 40\%~Cr (EMTO-CPA: 503, 488, 490~MHz), and reaches 513 at 50\% (513), 542 at 80\% and 580~MHz for AFM Cr (602). The bulk modulus has a local minimum at 40\%~Cr (163~GPa) where the Cr moments collapse, and the shear modulus flattens there. The experimental lattice parameter thus reproduces the magnitude of $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}$ and a magnetic anomaly near Fe--36Cr, but not the Fe-rich maximum near 20\%~Cr of the EMTO-CPA curve, so it gives a plateau rather than a minimum. At the PBE-relaxed volume $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}$ rises steadily from 504 to 591~MHz. The bulk-modulus drops at 80\% and 100\%~Cr reflect antiferromagnetic (Cr) and ferrimagnetic (Fe--80Cr, Cr antiparallel to Fe, net 0.31~$\mu_{\mathrm B}$/atom) order, which collapses $C_{12}$ as in pure Cr.

\subsection*{\DIFadd{S6. Electronic topological transition and the Fe-rich elastic anomaly}}
\DIFadd{The Fe-rich anomaly of the elastic constants (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_fe_rich}) coincides with a change of the spin-up Fermi-surface topology (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_fermi_unfolded}; Ref.~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{korzhavyi2009electronic}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
). It can be described quantitatively as an electronic topological transition (ETT) in the sense of Lifshitz \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{lifshitz1960anomalies,varlamov1989kinetic}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
. Let $E_c$ be the energy of the critical point, here the triply degenerate majority-spin level at H, and $z=E_{\mathrm F}-E_c$. When $z$ changes sign, the electronic grand potential acquires a non-analytic term,
}\begin{equation}
\DIFadd{\delta\Omega(z) = -c_L\,|z|^{5/2}\,\theta(z), \qquad \delta N(E_{\mathrm F}) \propto z^{1/2}\theta(z),
\label{eq:ett_omega}
}\end{equation}
\DIFadd{so that the singular part of an elastic constant, a second strain derivative of the energy, is
}\begin{equation}
\DIFadd{\delta C_{ij} = \frac{\partial^2 \delta\Omega}{\partial\varepsilon_i\,\partial\varepsilon_j} \simeq -\tfrac{15}{4}\,c_L\,\frac{\partial z}{\partial\varepsilon_i}\frac{\partial z}{\partial\varepsilon_j}\, z^{1/2}\,\theta(z) + O\!\left(z^{3/2}\right).
\label{eq:ett_c}
}\end{equation}
\DIFadd{This is the band term $C_{\rm band}\propto-\sum_k(\partial\varepsilon_k/\partial\eta)^2\delta(\varepsilon_k-E_{\mathrm F})$ of the new Fermi-surface sheet. Alloying shifts the critical level approximately linearly, $z\simeq\kappa(x-x_c)$, and substitutional disorder broadens it. Every modulus and the SAW frequency therefore follow
}\begin{equation}
\DIFadd{Q(x) = a + b\,x + A\left[\mathrm{Re}\sqrt{x-x_c+i\Gamma}-\mathrm{Re}\sqrt{-x_c+i\Gamma}\right],
\label{eq:ett_fit}
}\end{equation}
\DIFadd{a smooth background plus a square-root anomaly whose onset $x_c$ and width $\Gamma$ are common to all quantities; $a$, $b$ and $A$ differ between them. The sign of $A$ depends on the modulus, because at fixed electron number the shift of $E_{\mathrm F}$ also enters. The test of this description is whether one onset, fixed independently by the electronic structure, accounts for every data set (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_ett}).
}

\textit{\DIFadd{Onset from our electronic structure.}} \DIFadd{In ferromagnetic Fe the critical level lies $0.149$ eV above $E_{\mathrm F}$ at the PBE-relaxed lattice parameter and $0.104$ eV above it at the experimental one, so $\partial z/\partial\ln V\simeq1.07$ eV. In the unfolded bands of the SQS at the experimental lattice parameter (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_bands_unfolded_aexp}), the weight of this level at H is centred $0.005$ eV above $E_{\mathrm F}$ at 5\%~Cr ($z=-0.005$ eV) and $0.009$ eV below it at 10\%~Cr ($z=+0.009$ eV), with disorder spreads of $0.11$ and $0.10$ eV. A straight line through the three experimental-lattice points gives $\kappa=1.13$ eV and $x_c=0.079$, with the spread corresponding to $\pm0.10$ in composition (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_ett}a). The level reaches $E_{\mathrm F}$ already at 5\%~Cr, so the onset may lie nearer 0.05--0.07 than the linear estimate. At the PBE-relaxed lattice parameter (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_bands_unfolded_relaxed}) the level lies 0.065 and 0.068 eV above $E_{\mathrm F}$ at 5 and 10\%~Cr and approaches it more slowly. A straight line through the three relaxed-lattice points gives $\kappa=0.81$ eV and $x_c=0.17$ ($\pm0.15$), an extrapolation beyond the composition range of the bands. The experimental-lattice estimate is the one compared with the measured moduli below.
}

\textit{\DIFadd{Onset from the elastic constants.}} \DIFadd{A joint fit of Eq.~(\ref{eq:ett_fit}) to the EMTO-CPA $C_{44}$, $B$, $C'$ and $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}$ of Ref.~\mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{razumovskiy2011first} }\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
($0\le x\le0.2$), with one shared $x_c$ and $\Gamma$, gives $x_c=0.102$ (0.093--0.108 at $\Delta\chi^2\le1$) and $\Gamma=0.056$. The RMS residuals are 1.2, 2.9 and 0.19 GPa and 1.8 MHz (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_ett}b--e). Fixing $x_c$ and $\Gamma$ to the electronic-structure values instead fits all four quantities nearly as well: RMS 1.2, 3.3 and 0.19 GPa and 1.5 MHz, $\Delta\chi^2=3.1$ (within two standard deviations of the best fit). The model-independent markers fall in the same window: the knee of the EMTO-CPA $C_{44}$ at $x=0.059$, the minimum of the EMTO-CPA bulk modulus at 0.109, and the minimum of the }\emph{\DIFadd{measured}} \DIFadd{room-temperature bulk modulus at 0.068 \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{korzhavyi2009electronic}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
. A secondary minimum of the profile near $x_c\approx0.02$ ($\Delta\chi^2=1.7$), driven by the initial rise of $C_{44}$, is disfavoured. Our QE $C_{44}$ and $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}$ show the same rise and flattening between 0 and 10\%~Cr (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_ett}b,d). Only the onset is tested. The amplitudes $A$ are not interpreted, because over this window the background and the singular term are not uniquely separable.
}

\textit{\DIFadd{Link to the stiffening on decomposition.}} \DIFadd{The Fe-rich $\alpha$ phase of decomposed Fe--36Cr ($x_\alpha=0.12$ in MD, 0.14 by CALPHAD at 748~K \mbox{%DIFAUXCMD
\cite{xiong2011improved}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD
) lies beyond $x_c$ in the experimental-lattice and elastic-constant estimates (0.08--0.10), though not in the relaxed-lattice extrapolation (0.17), so decomposition carries the Fe-rich phase across the transition. Splitting the lever-rule gain of Fe--36Cr into the two phases (CALPHAD compositions) gives $+7.8\%$ for the electronic EMTO-CPA $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}(x)$, of which $\alpha$ contributes 2.4 and $\alpha'$ 5.4 percentage points, and $+5.0\%$ for the classical MD $f_{\mathrm{SAW}}(x)$ (1.4 and 3.6 points; Fig.~\ref{fig:S_ett}f). With the MD phase compositions the totals are $+7.7\%$ and $+4.0\%$, the $\alpha$ terms 2.0 and 1.4 points. The Fe-rich side, where the ETT sits, thus accounts for about 1.0 (0.6) of the 2.8 (3.7) percentage points by which the electronic stiffening exceeds the classical one; the remainder comes from the Cr-rich side. The ETT is therefore a measurable but secondary contributor to the stiffening that TGS detects.
}

\cite{backus1962long} }\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD

(b) An edge dislocation in a random Fe--Cr lattice: its energy, $\propto Gb^2\ln(R/r_0)$, is stored mostly in the Hookean far field, so slip resistance scales with $Gb$. (d) The relaxed 40-atom QE cells of an $\alpha$-like (20\% Cr) and an $\alpha'$-like (95\% Cr) composition with their local moments: $G$ rises from 96 to 120 GPa (PBE-relaxed; 88 to 107 GPa at the experimental lattice parameter), while their individual $K/G$ (1.98, 2.15; 1.81, 1.97) stays above Pugh's boundary. Molecular row. (e) The $25^3$ MD cells of Fe--36Cr, random ($\phi=0.5$) and fully decomposed ($\phi=1$). (f) A dislocation bowing between $\alpha'$ domains on the decomposed MD plane: the $\alpha/\alpha'$ shear-modulus misfit is $+23$ GPa with the EMTO-CPA moduli, $+16$ and $+21$ GPa with our QE sets (PBE-relaxed, experimental lattice parameter), but about $+5$ GPa with MD, and the lattice misfit is 0.39\%. (g) MD Fe--36Cr: with increasing conversion $\phi$, $G$ rises by 11\%, $K$ stays within 1\% and $K/G$ falls by 11\%. Data and TGS. \cite{pettifor1992theoretical}}\hskip0pt%DIFAUXCMD

EMTO-CPA moves from ductile Fe into that quadrant on the Cr-rich side. Of the tensors of this work, VASP, MD and the PBE-relaxed QE set stay ductile; the QE set at the experimental lattice parameter (filled blue diamonds) approaches Pugh's line between 20 and 50\% Cr ($K/G$ 1.78--1.88) and enters the doubly brittle quadrant at 80\% Cr ($K/G=1.42$, $C_{12}-C_{44}=-28$ GPa), where antiparallel Cr moments soften $C_{12}$ as in AF Cr (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_mechanism}d); the MD paths from random to fully decomposed (20, 36, 50\% Cr; open squares) all point towards brittle without crossing either line. (k) What TGS sees: the peak moves from 451 to 474 MHz while the flow stress rises ($\propto Gb$) and the plastic range shrinks (schematic stress--strain curves). Strain energy (bottom row). The elastic energy density $\Psi(K,G)=\frac{1}{2}K(\mathrm{tr}\,\varepsilon)^2+G\,\varepsilon':\varepsilon'$ links every step. (l) On decomposition of Fe--36Cr (MD) the volumetric stiffness changes by $-1.2\%$, the shear stiffness by $+11.0\%$ and Cahn's coherency modulus $Y=18KG/(3K+4G)$ by $+6.6\%$. (m) The coherency strain energy $2\eta^2Y\Omega$ is about 2 meV/atom for all tensors ($\eta=0.63\%$), three orders of magnitude below the chemical curvature, so elasticity barely shifts the Fe--Cr spinodal. (n) Mechanism, read left to right: decomposition changes the shear channel of $\Psi$ ($G$ $+11.0\%$; $\alpha'$ is stiffer in shear) but hardly the volume channel ($K$ $-1.2\%$), so $K/G$ falls by 11.0\%. The shear channel sets the TGS frequency ($f=\eta_R(\nu)\sqrt{G/\rho}/\lambda$, $+5.0\%$) and the slip resistance ($\propto Gb^2$, $+11.0\%$); the volume channel sets cleavage ($\propto Ka$, $-1.2\%$); $K/G$ sets ductility ($\varepsilon_F\propto(K/G)^2$, $-20.7\%$). Because $K$ is nearly fixed, the TGS shift reads embrittlement directly, $\Delta(K/G)/(K/G)\approx-2\,\Delta f/f$ (dashed). Coherency ($Y$ $+6.6\%$; about 2 meV/atom against about 1400 meV/atom of chemical curvature) feeds back on the decomposition by only about 0.1\%. Pugh's relations are empirical correlations for pure metals; the numbers indicate direction and scale, not a prediction for CASS.
